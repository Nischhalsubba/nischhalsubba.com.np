## The project and my part in it

My contribution to Designerex focused on browsing and listing patterns within the wider marketplace. Garment photography attracts attention, while size, availability and rental information help someone decide whether an item fits their plans.

The design challenge was the relationship between those two jobs. The catalogue needs room for the clothes without making practical information hard to find.

### What shaped the design

- Garment photographs vary in proportion and visual weight.

- Comparable rental details need consistent placement.

- Subtle navigation and filters still need clear interaction feedback.

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

## How can fashion discovery stay expressive while making rental decisions dependable?

I organized the story around the move from noticing a garment to comparing it. The repeated card pattern carries the essentials, while deeper detail supports a closer decision.

01 / Let the photograph introduce the garment

## Let the photograph introduce the garment

The image is the natural starting point in a fashion catalogue. Keeping practical details in a consistent area beneath it lets the photograph stay prominent while the renter begins to compare.

A card cannot carry every rental condition. I focused the browsing pattern on the information someone needs to decide whether an item is worth opening.

Too much metadata overwhelms the photographs; too little makes every comparison require another page.

02 / Keep comparable details in predictable places

## Keep comparable details in predictable places

A renter should not have to search a different part of each card for the same kind of information. Stable placement and labels make repeated listings useful as a comparison tool.

The detail page can then carry the fuller explanation. I would review the pattern with long garment names, different image proportions and unavailable options, since those are where a neat catalogue can become hard to use.

Consistency helps scanning, while the detail view gives each listing the room it needs.

03 / Preserve the browsing context

## Preserve the browsing context

Filters and navigation should help someone work through the catalogue and return to the place they left. The quiet visual treatment can support that work without hiding the controls.

Keyboard focus, active-filter feedback and the route back from a listing would be part of my next review. A premium-looking grid still needs predictable behavior.

Subtle controls need enough contrast and feedback to remain easy to use.

## Outcomes and next steps

The design work concerns image-led discovery and practical comparison. I do not claim ownership of the whole marketplace or measured booking improvements.

### What I would test next

- Ask a renter to compare two garments for occasion, size, price and availability without relying on memory.

- Review a long garment name, an unavailable date and a missing photograph to check whether the rental decision remains understandable.

### What I learned

The photograph starts the interest. A dependable listing pattern helps someone decide whether that interest fits their actual plans.
