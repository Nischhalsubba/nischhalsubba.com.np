A furniture starter site serves both a shopper and the person maintaining the catalogue. I contributed storefront hierarchy, reusable sections, product cards, typography and spacing for the WordPress/Elementor context.

The challenge was keeping an image-led catalogue coherent when photographs, product names and the amount of content vary.

### What shaped the design

- Furniture photography varies in crop and aspect ratio.

- Product names and catalogue size change after the demo is delivered.

- Site owners need editing freedom with clear spacing and type rules.

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

## How can a furniture starter site remain coherent after a site owner replaces the demo content?

I treated the storefront as a reusable pattern. The demonstration needs to look good, and the rules need to survive ordinary catalogue editing.

01 / Give product photographs room to vary

## Give product photographs room to vary

Furniture images differ in proportions and visual weight. A repeatable image area helps the catalogue read as one collection, but its crop rule needs care.

A fixed crop can create a neat rhythm while hiding part of a product. Containing the whole image preserves the object but can leave uneven space. I would choose the rule against varied product photography, not only the ideal demo images.

The most even grid is not always the clearest view of the product.

02 / Keep each product card easy to read

## Keep each product card easy to read

Product names and decision details need predictable positions. A longer name or a missing optional detail should not make a card feel like a different component.

For the reusable template, I would check wrapping, missing images, different product counts and narrow layouts. Those are normal content conditions for a site owner.

A stable card pattern needs room for ordinary variation.

03 / Make editing part of the design

## Make editing part of the design

A site owner needs to know which sections can be repeated, removed or rearranged. Simple spacing and type rules help preserve the page while leaving useful editing freedom.

That is why I describe the contribution as a starter-site interface and its content behavior. The initial storefront is one example of how the template can be used.

Too much freedom can produce visual drift; overly rigid sections make routine editing harder.

## What I delivered

A reusable storefront interface and CMS-aware content patterns, shown with the official published starter-site reference.

## Outcomes and next steps

The case explains catalogue and template decisions. Checkout, stock accuracy, fulfillment and sales performance were not verified.

### What I would test next

- Replace demo content with long product names, inconsistent photograph proportions and missing descriptions; check that the catalogue hierarchy survives.

- Ask a site owner to edit a category and product card, then inspect the result on a narrow screen without manually repairing the layout.

### What I learned

A useful template has to survive its second set of content. Long names and varied photographs belong in the design review from the start.
