## Problem framing

The original work is an unofficial airline landing-page and rebranding exploration. The design opportunity for an airline website is to communicate brand identity while helping travelers quickly understand the relevant travel offering and next action. This framing is a retrospective design hypothesis, not a documented client brief.

## My contribution and approach

My documented deliverable is the original visual identity and website concept created in Figma. The two original artwork images above show the scope of the exploration. The proposed approach for further development is to connect brand presentation to traveler decision-making, prioritize relevant information and distinguish marketing content from booking-related actions.

## Proposed traveler journey

1. Arrive: understand the airline and available travel offering.
2. Explore: find relevant routes, destination information and travel dates.
3. Evaluate: understand what is included, eligibility and fare conditions.
4. Proceed: identify the booking entry point and review the next step.
5. Recover: find support when a route is unavailable or a booking cannot be completed.

This is a proposed journey for validating a future implementation, not a claim that the original concept contains a complete transactional booking system.

## Proposed design decisions and trade-offs

- Keep brand imagery expressive without obscuring navigation or travel-related actions.
- Use predictable visual hierarchy to distinguish primary and secondary actions.
- If a booking form is introduced, keep origin, destination, dates and passenger information clear and validate inputs before submission.
- Communicate unavailable routes, changed fares, invalid dates and service errors in plain language.
- Preserve legibility and keyboard navigation across desktop and mobile layouts.

## Proposed research and validation

Ask prospective travelers to find relevant destination information, identify how to begin booking, explain the fare conditions they expect to see, and locate help. These questions test discoverability, comprehension, expectations and recovery respectively. Evaluate task completion and confidence, check contrast, focus, touch targets, accessible date inputs and localization.

## Outcome and limitations

The verified outcome is the published independent design concept, originally shared in April 2021. No commissioned engagement, booking integration, participant research or commercial performance improvement is documented. The next step would be to validate the travel-information hierarchy and transaction journey using an interactive prototype.
