Designing Web3 Products Without Making Users Feel Lost

What Yarsha and Mokshya taught me about wallet clarity, transaction review, trust language, and product education.

Working on Yarsha and Mokshya made me think about Web3 UX in a simple way: the interface has to make people feel informed before it asks them to act.

In a normal app, users usually understand what happens when they send a message, submit a form, or tap a button. In a Web3 product, one action can include wallet connection, signing, fees, network status, and transaction confirmation. If those details appear too late, the product starts to feel risky.

The signing screen should not be the first explanation

For Yarsha, I thought a lot about how chat, wallet actions, blinks, and transfers could feel connected without hiding important details. Users should know who they are sending to, what they are sending, what fee may apply, and what happens next before the final confirmation.

Trust language matters

Web3 products often use terms experienced users understand, but newer users may not. The answer is not to remove technical words completely. The better answer is layered language: simple meaning first, technical detail when needed.

Web3 design is product education

Mokshya.io needed a different kind of clarity. The challenge was public product storytelling: explain what the protocol is, why it matters, and where developers or users should go next.

Good Web3 UX does not hide complexity. It introduces it at the right time.

That is the kind of Web3 design I want to keep practicing: interfaces that feel modern, but still respect the user’s uncertainty.
