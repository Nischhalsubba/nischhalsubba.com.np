## Findings and implemented changes

1. **CRM masking, high priority.** Large opaque rectangles obscured the table while some names remained visible between rows. Replaced the published list with an original-source ownership-toolbar detail. No sensitive values were revealed. Removed old derivative bytes, including social cards.
2. **Related masking, high priority.** Recropped creditor status, Yarsha empty results and Zapp completion to exclude sensitive regions completely. Retired the Yarsha balance list, transaction detail and PiHub investor product illustration where a useful privacy-safe replacement was not verified. Their private originals remain untouched; no replacement screen is fabricated.
3. **Misleading presentation, medium priority.** Updated alt text, captions, case prose and article placements to distinguish focused details from full screens and privately reviewed source material from published images. Current totals: 16 curated images, 50 derivative files, 35 placements across 7 cases and 17 articles. Counts decreased deliberately because unusable pictures were removed.
4. **Credential mismatch, medium priority.** Serif blockquotes and visible `gap 24 / scroll snap` annotations made credential records look like testimonials and exposed implementation vocabulary. Replaced them with sans-serif records using the homepage's restrained dark surface, stronger title/date hierarchy, consistent spacing and retained keyboard-accessible horizontal controls. Removed the quote ornament and spacing annotations.
5. **Proof visibility, medium priority.** The homepage's three annual award records now display the unchanged original issuer SVGs already verified in the repository, linked to the Uxcel profile. The 2023 artwork still says Top 10; the accompanying third-place statement remains separately supported by the earlier issuer confirmation. Certifications retain their public verification links; no unofficial certificate artwork is invented. A fresh Gmail search returned RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED/PERMISSION_DENIED; no additional email assets were obtained in this review.
6. **Reading interruption, medium priority.** Long chapter headings were temporarily replaced by partial decoding glyphs. Retained the brand treatment on H1, but body H2 headings render complete text immediately across inner pages. This reduces repeated motion and avoids hiding content while a visitor reads.
7. **Senior-level editorial judgment.** Added two project-specific proposed evaluation tasks to every case. They connect the design argument to observable behavior and expose failure/recovery questions without inventing research results. Existing contribution boundaries, decision transitions and trade-offs remain. Reflections for Mokshya and Designerex now discuss design judgment rather than repeatedly foregrounding missing files.
