# Writing and editorial reference guide (v4)

## Research truth
- `https://bishal.cc/`: accessible About page, conversational, human introductions. A working on-domain `blog/`, `writing/`, or `thoughts/` page was **not verified**.
- `https://medium.com/@bishaller`: externally published Bishal writing exists; relevant articles include `The Career Trap: Why Staying Too Long at One Job Could Hurt You` (Jan 6 2025) and `Why do you fail as a Frontend Developer?` (June 10 2020). Texts use experience, direct problem framing, distinct sections and candid reflection.
- `https://nirajs.com.np/`: homepage and case-study pages (Visora, Zavfit, Vesuvio) were accessible; project narratives use project role, problem, research/UX approach, process and solution evidence. This is not necessarily a blog-writing model.

## What to borrow; what not to copy
Borrow: a personable first-person voice when actual experience supports it, upfront point of view, direct examples, specific design choices and trade-offs, short sections that earn their place and honest conclusions. Copy no phrase or analogy from other writers and do not misrepresent Nischhal's employment/research experience.

## Editorial North Star
Write like a senior product designer who can explain why a decision was necessary: not like an outsourced SEO checklist, a tutorial compiled from other articles, or generated generic advice. Name the screen, state, team, user, constraint and decision that actually existed. A first-person sentence must be backed by the project record. Do not use fictitious user quotes or invented interview data. Avoid the repetitive pattern `Problem -> Solution -> Lessons` for every article; choose a shape suited to the actual argument.

## Good article structure (adapt, don't force)
- A sharp lead: What was hard, unexpected or costly for the users?
- Specific context: Which project or class of flows? Who had the decision? What constraints are known?
- Distinct thesis: One concrete claim, not five keyword-stuffed promises.
- Real visual evidence: screenshot(s) of actual Figma/app/site/implementation, a source-backed caption and accessible alt.
- Analysis: What option was considered, why a particular state/copy/system was chosen; separate facts from proposed tests.
- Practical takeaway: A usable technique tied to the actual example, not padded generic best-practice paragraphs.
- Further reading: 1 relevant project page + 1 related article; no arbitrary ten-link blocks.

## Good case-study structure
Project summary, product purpose, user's role/ownership and time period, constraints, problem, actual journey screenshots, design decisions and alternatives, implementation/handoff evidence, observed outcome if verified, limitations/open questions, related work and contact CTA. For multi-role apps, walk a complete journey rather than arrange a random image gallery. For website case studies, preserve full website compositions and label historical/starter demos accurately.

## Original micro-examples (proposals, not historical facts)
### Instead of generic handoff advice
Weak: “Good design handoff improves collaboration and reduces confusion.”
Possible original opening: “A button can be perfectly drawn in Figma and still leave an engineer with five unanswered questions: what happens when the request fails, when the role lacks permission, or when the screen loses connection? This note shows the states I'd document before calling a design ready.”
This is a generic educational statement. If presented as a personal project anecdote, verify the source first.

### Instead of generic empty-state advice
Weak: “Empty states help users have a better experience.”
Possible original opening: “A blank table is rarely one problem. It might mean a new workspace, a restrictive filter, missing permissions, or a failed request. The interface should tell people which one they're looking at.”
Link to a genuine corresponding screenshot and discuss only states present in the actual design.

### For Zakra Furniture
Use the supplied full screenshot to explain how the homepage established clear product navigation and a focused furniture hero. Do not write that an e-commerce conversion rate increased or the project was a production furniture retailer unless separately verified.

## Article QA
For each of the 27 canonical articles, complete `question`, `actual intended audience`, `evidence files`, `distinct thesis`, `personally verified experience`, `authentic hero image`, `alt/caption`, `no repeated cards`, `sources`, `canonical`, `published/mod date truth`, `related page`, `quality verdict`, `blocker`. No invented minimum word count or keyword-density threshold.
