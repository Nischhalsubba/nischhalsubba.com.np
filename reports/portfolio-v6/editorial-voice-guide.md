# Nischhal editorial voice

Write for a person inspecting a design decision, not for a word-count target. State the concrete question early, show the artifact that makes the question inspectable, and explain what remains uncertain. An article should leave the reader with a useful review action.

Use first person for a contribution supported by the portfolio, résumé or source record. Do not turn a sensible recommendation into a remembered project event. ‘I would test whether the recipient remains clear’ is a proposed evaluation; ‘I tested this with users’ requires an actual research record. A screenshot can establish a visible state, not a backend guarantee or business result.

Build a paragraph around a specific tension. Name what the design gains, what it makes harder, and the condition under which the choice needs revisiting. Keep related ideas together. Avoid opening every article with a generic claim about the importance of design, repeating a standard conclusion, or using a list of benefits in place of an explanation.

Treat sources as part of the explanation. A caption names the product, historical/current status and source boundary. A current marketing website is separate from a historical contribution. An external component example remains credited to its creator. Dummy values remain design content. Unknown dates, authorship or outcomes stay unknown.

## Original editing example

Earlier Mokshya wording: ‘An introductory path and a developer path can share one coherent story without requiring the same reading depth.’

Revised account: ‘The hero presents Explore and Start a project as different actions. The header also exposes Experiments and repeats Start a project. Those labels suggest two visitor intentions: inspect the work or begin a conversation. I can discuss their visibility and relative emphasis from the image without claiming to have tested where they lead.’

The revision names observable labels, connects them to the evaluation question and makes the verification limit explicit. It does not invent an interview, navigation test or conversion result.

## Review before publication

Check that the intended reader and question differ from the neighboring articles. Confirm that each specific example matches its source image and that the complete original is available. Verify the contribution, year and outcome limits. Keep one useful case or service link in context. Inspect the generated page and its narrow layout; structural or automated checks do not replace that reading.
